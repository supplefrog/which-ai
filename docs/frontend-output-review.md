User judgments are authoritative evidence of this user's taste for these examples. They are not universal design rules or a controlled estimate of skill quality. No candidate designs or live shared skills were changed during this review.

## What the user wants the design to achieve

The design should sell its idea. Typography, colour, space, imagery, outlines, gradients and movement should collectively express the product, with relationships that feel intuitive from real-world counterparts. Useful visuals integrated with the experience are preferred over scattered explanatory text. Every element needs a purpose; curiosity, delight and expression can supply that purpose as well as utility.

The user wants meaningful variety because different products and intentions need different treatments. Recolouring one structure, repeating an app window, or applying one entrance effect to every example does not provide enough choice. This is a preference for coherent alternatives, not random novelty inside one product.

## Additional rendered findings

1. **The comparison's intermediate width is material.** At 685px, baseline 1 retains a `172px 445px` hero grid, squeezing its headline into 172px and roughly 403px height. Anthropic stacks into one column, giving its headline 520px width and roughly 132px height. At 1440px, baseline is much more balanced. This confirms a responsive weakness that worsens the user's first-glance experience; it does not invalidate their taste judgment. [Baseline 685px](../output/design-review/local-baseline-1-685.png), [Anthropic 685px](../output/design-review/local-anthropic-1-685.png).
2. **Anthropic changes the way the idea is explained.** Folders, a growing network, loose notebook pages/books, a thought graph and a phone capture interface offer different product representations. Personal changes its hero metaphor but appends one unchanged demo below each. The latter's identical silhouette, content and outlines overwhelm differences on a full-page comparison. [Anthropic 1](../output/design-review/local-anthropic-1.png), [Anthropic 3](../output/design-review/local-anthropic-3.png), [personal 1](../output/playwright/personal-1-desktop.png), [personal 3](../output/playwright/personal-3-desktop.png).
3. **Colour preference is about relationships.** Anthropic's filled layers distinguish objects without outlining every boundary. Some admired depth comes from flat colour, overlap and soft shadows rather than literal gradients. The transferable quality is the coherent treatment of surfaces and light, not a requirement to add gradients everywhere.
4. **Small text is often compensation for an unclear visual.** Tiny captions label the metaphor, explain what to click, repeat the headline and restate benefits. The same information is then repeated in feature lists and closing slogans. Stronger visual explanation and contextual feedback would let those passages disappear. Real note titles, labels and honest local-demo boundaries still carry useful information.
5. **Hallmark has more macrostructure variety than its first-glance similarity suggests.** Its five pages include a notebook front, manifesto, letter, FAQ and index. Shared type choices, recurring symbols and one capture form still produce a visible family resemblance. Both observations can hold: distinct structures and insufficient perceived variety.
6. **Geometry defects undermine the central promise.** A “connected thinking” illustration with unattached lines cannot be treated as harmless decoration. The connector and object should share geometry through responsive changes, rather than being positioned independently and merely resembling a graph.
7. **The motion defects are visible in intermediate states.** GPT Taste 1 scales an image inside a fixed frame, exposing a surrounding surface before filling it; example 2 lets the incoming image cover previous chapter text. Emil 2's selector movement is clear, while its panel has only a subtle `.98 → 1` scale-in plus a fade/6px translation; the user's expected stronger zoom is not conveyed. Direct clicks on the depicted note content in Emil 1/4 do not select a note. Baseline 4's strip/underline are static. Taste 1–5 do have different motion modes despite their shared structures. These findings distinguish insufficient perceptible feedback from a literal absence of animation, and do not treat every perceived hesitation as a measured performance fault. See the [bounded motion review](../output/review-motion.md) and [paired zoom captures](../output/design-review/motion/taste1-scroll-2200-0.png), [850ms later](../output/design-review/motion/taste1-scroll-2200-850.png).

## Source findings and evaluation limits

- **Personal repetition is an implementation fact.** `Designs.tsx` defines one `Demo` at lines 12–34 and calls it on all five pages at lines 49–57. The same `Start` adds a corner arrow at line 42. Shared CSS fixes demo border, radius and shadow. The skill did not mandate these choices.
- **The current personal instructions already address several failures.** Frozen `SKILL.md` lines 64/70 prefer proximity before cards/borders/shadows, and flag ornamental numbering and repeated reveals. Personal defaults require boxes/dividers to serve a role and discourage generic quirky slogans. Another longer blacklist would duplicate advice without establishing better behavior.
- **Asset opportunities were not matched.** A readback of the personal worker's original dispatch explicitly permits native imagegen when the skill's reasoning calls for it; the worker chose not to generate assets. Anthropic excluded image generation. Taste v2 did generate three images and reused them across its five pages; GPT Taste used remote placeholder imagery. This run demonstrates that personal generation did not exercise the capability despite permission; it cannot establish that the image-generation route itself is ineffective. The parent initially misstated this as a harness prohibition and corrected that claim after dispatch readback.
- **GPT Taste variation was structurally limited.** Its skill prescribes an AIDA sequence and an architecture arsenal. The worker chose the same three architectures for all five, then used one `Design` component with theme data. This explains more than colour preference alone. Taste v2 also shares motion and demo components.
- **Vercel was a review-and-repair pass.** It preserved baseline composition while improving semantics, focus/dialog behavior and other interface details. Its lack of visual redesign is expected for that condition; it is not a matched generative design trial.
- **Five designs are correlated alternatives.** Each condition's five outputs came from one worker batch, not five independent trials. They inform this revision and the user's selection; they do not isolate model defaults from skill effects or support universal rankings.

## Revision target for the next candidate

These are project-local hypotheses for a revised skill, not live promoted instructions:

- Lead with the product idea and the desired experience. Let content, physical relationships and intended behavior determine composition, typography, colour and imagery.
- Make each requested concept distinct in structure and explanation as well as palette. Shared low-level behavior may remain accessible and correct without sharing a visible app-window template.
- Remove copy and chrome that add no information, affordance, expression or discovery. Use numbers for a real sequence/reference; choose symbols for their contextual meaning. Preserve meaningful labels and disclosures.
- Integrate the demonstration with the idea being sold. Let people act on the depicted object where that action makes sense; reveal state changes with appropriate feedback.
- Treat motion as part of the concept: handwriting/drawing, connection formation, card movement, a strip, or a reveal can explain something. Judge intermediate states, clipping, timing and interruption in playback. A universal fade-up is not a motion concept.
- Keep the design language open. Borders, gradients, shadows, stillness, serif type, dark green and playful movement all remain available when they fit. A rejection of one automatic treatment does not justify its automatic opposite.
- Exercise the permitted native asset route when an authored image would materially help. Keep asset permission matched in comparisons, record actual route/version, and avoid placeholder imagery that contributes no meaning.

A future acceptance review should judge whether the idea is clear at a glance, whether each alternative changes how it is expressed, whether small elements earn their space, and whether actual interaction reinforces the intended physical/visual relationships. Compare the candidate against the frozen personal run and the liked qualities in Anthropic/Impeccable/Addy; do not assume mixing more source skills improves the result.

Shared-skill revision and a new generation run remain the next step, separate from this evidence record. The reviewed outputs are preserved as the reference baseline.
